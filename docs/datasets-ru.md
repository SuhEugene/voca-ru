# Russian speech and text data for a small TTS model

Survey for `sultson/voca` (4.5M-param Rust TTS), target: small single-/few-speaker
Russian model at ~22.05 kHz, plus a small ASR set for round-trip WER.

**Verified:** 2026-11-19. Every URL below was fetched live on that date; HTTP
status is in the verification log at the end. Hours/speakers are from the
publisher (or from MFA's corpus metadata where the publisher is vague). Do not
treat remembered numbers as verified — the numbers here are the ones that were
on the live pages.

Legend for licence: **NC** = non-commercial, **SA** = share-alike,
**BY** = attribution. "Commercial?" answers whether a commercial product may
ship the *data or a model trained on it*; share-alike is called out separately
because model-weight obligations are legally unsettled.

> **Scope correction (2026-11-19):** the project now reproduces upstream's
> synthetic-data recipe — Russian audio is generated by Qwen3-TTS
> (VoiceDesign + Base clone), and the 4.5M model is trained on that. **Sections
> 1–5 are the primary path.** Sections 6–12 are the earlier recorded-corpus
> survey, retained as **fallback only**; they are not the training data for this
> project.

Russian, 5–30 words, and the text licence must permit redistribution in a
research dataset. Every row must carry `source`, `source_url`,
`source_license`, and (for literature) `author`/`work`/`year`.

**Recommended mix**

| batch | source | count | split |
|---|---|---:|---|
| 1 (4,500) | Common Voice ru `sentence-collector.txt` | 3,000 | 80/10/10 by sentence: 3,600 train / 450 val / 450 test |
| 1 | Wikisource PD prose | 900 | same split |
| 1 | LLM-written | 600 | same split |
| 2 (3,500) | project templates | 2,600 | **train only** |
| 2 | LLM-written with numbers/dates/money | 900 | **train only** |

Total **8,000 sentences**, a few MB of text. Freeze val/test after a
normalised-hash dedupe; batch 2 must be disjoint from batch 1. Match upstream's
mix: about **25% questions and 10% exclamations**, with the remainder statements.

**Licence consequences for a research release**

- The release can be **CC0 1.0** if it contains only CV (CC0), Wikisource PD
  text, and project-own LLM/template sentences. Attribute Wikisource authors in
  the provenance file (not legally required for PD, but good scholarly practice).
- Adding **Wikipedia** sentences forces the script list to **CC BY-SA 4.0**:
  attribution alone is not enough; a CC0 release would be non-compliant. Keep
  Wikipedia out of the released list.
- **OPUS/OpenSubtitles** has no clear licence; exclude it. News corpora have the
  same problem.
- LLM outputs are project-own for this purpose, but avoid prompting with
  copyrighted passages; generate fresh text and dedupe against the sources.

| 25 | abbreviation | `т. е. {statement}, а не {statement}; см. примечание на с. {n}.` | т. е. это возможно, а не обязательно; см. примечание на с. 12. | `т. е.` → «то есть»; `см.` → «смотри»; `с.` → «страница» |

**Stress traps these expose**

- **Cardinal government:** 1 → `рубль/копейка`; 2–4 → `рубля/копейки`;
  5–20 and compounds ending 0/5–9 → `рублей/копеек`; 11–14 → `рублей` even
  though they end in 1–4.
- **Dates:** ordinal in genitive (`пя́того`, `тридца́того`), month in genitive
  (`ма́я`, `ию́ня`), and the year phrase `в … году́` (prepositional) versus
  `… го́да` (genitive). `ruaccent`'s dictionary gives `г+оду` by default and
  lists `год+у` as an omograph; the context model must choose.
- **`полтора`/`полторы`:** masculine/neuter `полтора́`, feminine `полторы́`;
  the following noun is genitive singular (`часа́`, `мину́ты`).
- **Money:** `ру́бль` (nom.), `рубля́` (gen. sg.), `рубле́й` (gen. pl.);
  `копе́йка`, `копе́йки`, `копе́ек`.
- **Names:** dative `Ива́ну`, genitive `Ива́на`, instrumental `Петро́вым`;
  feminine surnames (`Петро́ва`) change stress/gender agreement.
- **Abbreviations/Latin:** `т. е.`, `см.`, `с.`, `ул.`, `г.`, `руб.` are
  ambiguous; `ИНН` and phone numbers should be spelled digit-by-digit, not read
  as huge cardinals.
- **Lexical probes:** `звони́т`, `позвони́шь`, `догово́р`, `катало́г`,
  `краси́вее`, `то́рты`, `ба́нты`, `свёкла`, `жалюзи́`, `шофёр`.

Do not let the TTS model guess numbers: normalise digits to Russian words in
the text normaliser (F3), then apply stress. The templates are the regression
suite for that normaliser.

## 3. Stress resources, deepened

### 3.1 `ruaccent` — dictionary **and** models

`ruaccent` is both:

- **Dictionary:** `dictionary/accents.json.gz` — **3,194,879 word forms**,
  21.0 MB gzipped / 175.5 MB raw JSON. Key = lowercase word, value = word with
  `+` immediately before the stressed vowel (`стол` → `ст+ол`, `рублей` →
  `рубл+ей`). Inflected forms are included (`стол/стола/столу/столом/столе/столы`
  all present).
- **Omograph dictionary:** `dictionary/omographs.json.gz` — **19,741 entries**,
  word → list of stressed variants (`замок` → `з+амок`, `зам+ок`; `году` →
  `г+оду`, `год+у`).
- **Neural models:** `nn/nn_accent/model.onnx` (~0.8 MB) for OOV stress;
  `nn/nn_omograph/tiny/model.onnx` (~10.5 MB) or `turbo2` (~359 MB) for
  context; tokenizer ~0.88 MB; plus `nn_stress_usage_predictor`,
  `nn_yo_homograph_resolver`, `dictionary/rule_engine`, and `koziev` morphology.
- **Python API:** `RUAccent().load(use_dictionary=True, omograph_model_size="tiny")`,
  then `process_all(text)`. `load` downloads the selected files from
  `ruaccent/accentuator`; it does not need the whole 6.26 GB repo.

**How it decides homographs:** dictionary lookup gives candidates; the ONNX
omograph model scores each candidate in sentence context (`OmographModel.classify`);
`yo_words`/`yo_homograph` handle `ё`; the stress-usage model decides whether a
word needs a mark at all. The dictionary alone is not enough for `году`/`года`
and `за́мок/замо́к`, which is why the model matters.

**Ship in Rust?** Yes, with a size decision:

| component | size | Rust plan |
|---|---:|---|
| `accents.json.gz` | 21.0 MB gz / 175.5 MB raw | Embed gz, decompress once into a trie/FST; or prune to the training vocabulary. A raw `HashMap` of 3.19M strings is ~150–250 MB RAM — avoid. |
| `omographs.json.gz` | 219 KB gz / ~1.5 MB raw | Embed directly. |
| `nn/nn_omograph/tiny` | 10.5 MB + 0.88 MB tokenizer | `ort` + `tokenizers` crates; ~11.4 MB. |
| `nn/nn_accent` | 0.8 MB | Same ONNX session, or skip if dictionary coverage is enough. |

Total shippable ≈ **33 MB compressed** (dictionary + omographs + tiny model +
tokenizer + accent model). That is larger than the 20 MB voca binary, so decide:
(a) ship the full 33 MB for best quality; (b) ship dictionary + omographs only
(~22 MB) and accept wrong `году`/`замок` in rare contexts; or (c) prune the
dictionary to the model's training vocabulary (~100k forms) and ship the tiny
model. **MIT** applies: include the copyright/permission notice in the binary
or distribution; no share-alike, no NC.

### 3.2 `russian_g2p` — stress + phonemes (MIT)

- `Accentor.do_accents` returns text with `+` before the stressed vowel
  (`конфере+нция`). It is a separate accentor; use it to cross-check ruaccent,
  not as the primary dictionary.
- `Grapheme2Phoneme` returns uppercase phoneme tokens. **`0` is overloaded**:
  on a vowel it means stressed (`O0`), on a consonant it means palatalised
  (`D0`). `sil` is silence. The practical inventory is **57 non-syllabic
  symbols** (29 base phones × stressed/unstressed or hard/soft) plus `sil`;
  the code's set has 115 including `l`-syllabic allophones. Example:
  `диало+г` → `D0 I A L O0 K`.
- Bundled data: `Accents.json` 54.7 MB, `All_Forms_UTF8.txt` 64.5 MB,
  homographs 2.4 MB, DAWG 1.5 MB. The repo `LICENSE` is MIT, but the bundled
  `corpus/` includes third-party-derived material (VoxForge/Wiktionary); verify
  provenance before redistributing the data. Using it locally to build the
  lexicon is lower risk.
- Old (2018) and rule-based; good for a compact lexicon, but verify its
  `0` semantics in the Rust frontend with a parity test.

### 3.3 eSpeak NG `ru` — inventory and GPL-3.0

- Voice definition: `espeak-ng-data/lang/zle/ru` (one line: `name Russian language ru`).
- Russian phoneme rules: `phsource/ph_russian` — **48 `phoneme` definitions**
  (e.g. `g, p, x, k, l, l^, s, s;, n_, n#, r, ts, dZ;, tS;, t;, d;, m;, I^`
  plus vowels `i, I#, y, I, I2, I3, E, E#, E2, E3, E%, o, O, u, u#, u", 8, e,
  ju, ja, ...`). Stress is the separate `'` phoneme; it is not encoded in the
  vowel symbol.
- **Licence: GPL-3.0.** Porting `ph_russian`, the Russian `ru_dict`, or eSpeak's
  rule code into the Rust frontend creates a GPL-3.0 derivative. Using the
  `espeak-ng` CLI only as an offline oracle for test labels is a different
  case, but get legal review before shipping. For the Apache-2.0 Rust binary,
  use **ruaccent + russian_g2p** (MIT) or write original rules; keep eSpeak as
  a cross-check only.

## 4. ASR judge

All three are Russian-capable; WER numbers below come from different harnesses,
so compare within a row/benchmark, not across.

| model | HF repo id | params | licence | Russian WER, read speech | Other Russian domains | Speed on M5/CPU | Memory (approx.) | verdict |
|---|---|---|---:|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Whisper large-v3** | `openai/whisper-large-v3` | 1.55B | **Apache-2.0** | FLEURS 3.1%, CV19 5.5%, RuLS 9.4% (GigaAM eval) | Golos crowd 19.0%, farfield 16.4% | Slowest; 1.55B | ~6 GB VRAM fp16 / ~3 GB int8; 8–12 GB RAM CPU | Accuracy reference; too heavy for 17k-clip screening |
| **Whisper large-v3-turbo** | `openai/whisper-large-v3-turbo` | 809M (4 decoder layers) | **MIT** | **FLEURS 4.44%** (n=775, independent benchmark); audiobooks 9.89% | spontaneous 7.73% | Fast; MLX/`faster-whisper`; no M5 number published | ~2–3 GB VRAM int8; ~2–4 GB RAM CPU | **Primary pick** |
| **GigaAM-v3 RNNT/CTC** | `ai-sage/GigaAM-v3`; ONNX `istupakov/gigaam-v3-onnx` | 220–240M | **MIT** | **FLEURS 4.50%** (same benchmark); RuLS 4.4%, CV19 0.9% (GigaAM eval) | audiobooks **6.57%**, spontaneous **6.95%** (wins both) | Lightest; ONNX CPU; no M5 RTF published | <1 GB RAM/VRAM | **Fallback / cross-check**; swap to it if CPU throughput is the bottleneck |

**Recommendation:** use **Whisper large-v3-turbo** for the 15%-WER screen and the
final eval. On clean read speech it is tied with GigaAM (4.44 vs 4.50, overlapping
CIs), it is MIT, and the harness is already in the F1 plan (`faster-whisper` /
MLX). Use **GigaAM-v3 RNNT** as the mandatory cross-check on ~200 clips; if turbo
and GigaAM disagree by more than ~2 points, or if the M5 becomes CPU-throughput
bound, switch the final eval to GigaAM. Do **not** use large-v3 by default: it is
the accuracy reference but costs ~2× the compute for at most a small,
harness-confounded read-speech gain.

Speed evidence: `faster-whisper` reports large-v2 int8 transcribing 13 min of
audio in 59 s on an 8-thread i7-12700K (RTF ≈ 0.076, ~13× real-time); turbo is
faster because its decoder is 4 layers, large-v3 is slower. No M5-specific RTF
is published for either Whisper or GigaAM — measure it in F1 and record it in
F5. Optional Whisper fine-tune: `antony66/whisper-large-v3-russian` (CV17 WER
9.84 → 6.39, but WIP, claims phone-call domain).

## 5. Qwen3-TTS Russian evidence

**Official**

- Qwen3-TTS-12Hz-1.7B-Base/VoiceDesign are **Apache-2.0** and officially list
  Russian among 10 languages. The model card shows only Chinese/English Seed-TTS
  WER; it does **not** publish a Russian stress or number eval.
- The technical report's multilingual table gives Russian WER **3.212%** for
  12Hz-1.7B-Base, 4.535% for 25Hz-1.7B, 4.458% for 12Hz-0.6B, 5.957% for
  25Hz-0.6B; MiniMax 4.281%, ElevenLabs 3.878%. Russian is among the weaker
  languages in that table (Japanese 3.823%; Russian 3.212%), but still
  competitive.
- No official Russian demo, and a GitHub issue search for “russian” found no
  Russian-quality report (four hits, none about pronunciation/stress).

**Third-party evidence (the useful part)**

Two Apache-2.0 Russian stress fine-tunes of the 1.7B Base model document the
base model's weakness:

- Base Qwen3-TTS follows a stress mark on the dictionary vowel only **65%** of
  the time and a mark *against* the dictionary **17%**; it largely ignores
  `U+0301`. On ordinary text its stress differs from RUAccent in **~14%** of
  words (detector has 10–12% false alarms and finds 71% of shifted stress), and
  its round-trip CER is **3.6%**.
- The worst cases are **English terms written in Cyrillic** (“экстендс”,
  “он делит каскад”), where RUAccent also guesses. There is **no report on
  numbers**; that must be tested with the templates above.
- The fine-tunes (`sknyazev/qwen3-tts-12hz-1.7b-ru-stress-gguf`,
  `siriusfreak/qwen3-tts-12hz-1.7b-ru-stress-cf`) follow marks at 96–100% and
  are trained on Russian LibriSpeech; both are Apache-2.0. The voicy demo is
  <https://olluorg.github.io/voicy/stress/>.

**Implication for this project:** base Qwen3-TTS's ~15% stress error is exactly
at our 15% screening threshold, so an unfiltered teacher will bake in errors that
the WER screen does not catch. Either (a) use a stress-following Russian
fine-tune as the teacher, or (b) keep the Base model but normalise numbers and
apply `U+0301` marks and verify with the stress detector; base ignores marks, so
(b) only works with a fine-tune that follows them. Test both on 200 clips before
generating 17k.

### TTS notes

- **RUSLAN is the only true studio single-speaker Russian TTS corpus found.**
  31.5 h at 44.1 kHz clears the “5–50 h” bar for a 4.5M-param model with room
  to spare. It is NC-SA, so the orchestrator must ask the human before use.
- **RuLS is the best licence-clean substitute**: public domain in the USA,
  98 h / 13 readers, and `text_no_preprocessing` already has case and
  punctuation. Pick the reader with the most utterances; 7–15 h is the
  expected range, which is still enough.
- **M-AILABS** is the best “few-speaker, ~15 h per speaker” option, but its
  licence is contradictory (2020 permissive relicense vs CC BY-NC-ND on
  mirrors). Do not ship it without a human decision.
- **Golos / SOVA / Common Voice** are ASR-style corpora: lowercase,
  unpunctuated transcripts and variable acoustics. Useful for pretraining or
  ASR, not for a clean single-speaker TTS front end.
- The RUSLAN derivatives (`akuzdeuov/ruslan-tts`, `stilletto/ruslan-stressed`,
  `intexcp/ruslan_sova_ai`, `Diana2di/ruslan_data`) are **not** independently
  licensed. Treat them as RUSLAN (NC-SA) or avoid.

### Stress / G2P recommendation

Use **ruaccent (MIT)** as the stress layer and **russian_g2p (MIT)** as the
phoneme layer. Both are permissively licensed and self-contained; together they
cover the training-text vocabulary and produce a stress-marked phoneme lexicon
without touching NC data. `ruaccent`'s 3.19M-form dictionary is more valuable
for Russian TTS quality than adding another 50 h of unaccented audio.

Practical pipeline:

1. Normalise text (case, punctuation, `ё`).
2. Add stress with `ruaccent` dictionary + omograph model.
3. Convert stressed text to phonemes with `russian_g2p`.
4. Train the acoustic model on phoneme or character+stress tokens.

Do **not** train a stress predictor from scratch unless the off-the-shelf
dictionary shows a coverage gap on the target corpus; 3.19M forms is already
larger than the vocabulary of any 31 h corpus.

## 12. Notes for the download/preprocessing scripts

- **Target rate:** resample to 22.05 kHz, mono, 16-bit PCM. RUSLAN at 44.1 kHz
  is an exact 2:1 decimation (low-pass first). RuLS/M-AILABS/Golos/SOVA are
  16 kHz; upsampling adds no information but keeps one pipeline.
- **Trim/pad:** RUSLAN already has leading/trailing silence removed. RuLS is
  segmented; trim to ~0.1 s silence. Golos far-field has long silences.
- **Transcripts:** for TTS use `text_no_preprocessing` (RuLS) or the official
  RUSLAN text; do not use Golos/SOVA lowercase ASR transcripts as TTS text
  without restoration. Common Voice prompts do have case+punctuation, but the
  audio is crowd-quality.
- **Stress:** apply ruaccent at preprocessing time and store the stressed text
  next to the original. Do not modify the original transcripts in place.
- **Leakage:** if RuLS is used for training, exclude RuLS test from the WER
  judge. Prefer FLEURS test as the judge because its speakers are disjoint from
  train/dev.
- **Disk:** `data/` is gitignored; keep raw archives out of git. Convert to
  22.05 kHz and delete the originals once checksums pass.
